The Real Math on Storage Websites: Rented Template vs. Owned

I own seven storage facilities in Minnesota and Wisconsin. I've paid for the rented website. I've paid the marketing guy. Then I learned to build it myself — and this is the comparison I wish someone had shown me first.

A software-company website is a rented apartment: decent walls, you can't renovate, and the rent never ends. An owned website is a house — built once, keys handed to you, and your only bill forever is about $12 a year for the address.

What the rented website does well — credit where due

The template sites from the big storage-software companies (StorEdge, Storable, SiteLink and similar) get three real things right:

  • It exists by Friday. Fill out a form, get a website. No decisions, no builder to find.
  • Rentals are wired in. Unit inventory, online move-ins, and payments connect natively, because the website and the management software are the same company.
  • Someone else worries about it. Hosting, security, and updates are handled, and there's a support line to call.

If those three things were the whole story, I'd tell you to keep the template. They're not.

The honest caveat about custom websites

A custom website is only better if it's built and maintained by someone competent. A bad custom build — the web guy who disappears, the site you don't control — is the worst option of all. I know because I lived it: one of my facilities had the wrong phone number on a national directory for years and the guy I was paying never noticed.

That's exactly why my builds work the way they do: you get the code, the accounts are opened in your name, hosting is free, and the site is static — nothing to break, nothing held hostage, no bill that can lapse. You keep your management software (StorEdge, CC Storage, SiteLink — whatever you run) exactly as it is. The site just plugs into it.

Three questions to ask any website provider

  • If I stop paying you, does my website still exist tomorrow?
  • Can you show me the code that tells Google my prices, hours, and location — and can I take it with me?
  • When someone searches for storage in my town and the towns around me, which page of yours ranks — and can we build a page for each?

A rented-template provider answers no, no, and no. That's the whole conversation.
